\section{Experimental Study}
\label{sec-exp}

We evaluate \hermes on three questions.
\textbf{[RQ1] Effectiveness}: how accurate is operator-level planning
against one-shot generation and constraint-guided search, at the same
model and on the same graph store?
\textbf{[RQ2] Components}: what do the verifier, the execution feedback,
and the reference answers contribute?
\textbf{[RQ3] Cost}: how many model calls, graph executions, and how
much time does each method spend?
All configurations and run outputs are in the artifact.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\stitle{Benchmarks}.
We normalize six benchmarks into one format
(Table~\ref{tab-bench}): every question and language becomes one item
with its gold query, feature tags (\eg \kw{COUNT}, \kw{GROUP},
\kw{ORDER}, \kw{ASK}), and gold answers stored as in the official
Text2SPARQL scorer.
Sources are pinned to commits and checksums.
This paper reports CK26 and DB26, the two tracks of the Text2SPARQL 2026
challenge~\cite{text2sparql}.
CK26 asks 50 questions in English and German over a corporate graph of
products, suppliers, and employees (13 classes, 30 properties, about
26.6k triples), loaded locally; DB26 asks 50 questions in English and
Spanish over DBpedia~\cite{auer2007dbpedia}, queried at the organizers'
endpoint.
Each track has 100 items.

\stitle{Data caveats}.
Three properties of the data shape what a number means.
(1) Forty-nine of the fifty CK26 gold queries appear verbatim in CK25, on
the same graph, so neither is a development set for the other; no
method here uses either for examples or tuning.
(2) The CK gold answers were computed on the Tentris engine.
Rerunning the gold queries on Oxigraph reproduces 43 of the 50; the
other seven differ in tie-breaking under \kw{LIMIT}, decimal formatting,
or the non-standard cast \kw{xsd{:}int()}.
We report results on all items and on the 86 items whose gold
reproduces.
All 50 DB26 gold queries reproduce on the organizers' endpoint.
(3) The official scorer has two quirks for ASK and ordered questions,
described in Appendix~C; we report the official metric and a strict
variant that corrects them.

\stitle{Metrics}.
We report set precision (P), recall (R), and $F_1$, and the challenge's
ranking metric $F_{\mathrm{ndcg}}$, which uses nDCG on questions whose
order matters and set $F_1$ elsewhere.
Our scorer reproduces all 5,024 per-question and average scores
published for the 2026 challenge (Appendix~C), so our numbers are on
the leaderboard's scale.

\stitle{Methods}.
\emph{OneShot} prompts a single query from the schema vocabulary,
declares any known prefix the draft omits, and makes one repair call if
the draft does not parse.
\emph{CGS} is our reimplementation of constraint-guided search with
Chase and Backchase~\cite{wang2026uniqgen}.
It follows the published three stages, with two documented deviations:
queries are rendered from constraints deterministically rather than by
a second model, and candidates are scored by comparing answer sets with
the reference answers rather than by a model.
It uses two model calls per question, one for the constraint table and
one for the reference answers, and beam width 5.
\emph{\hermes{}$^-$} is the planner of Section~\ref{sec-framework} with
the verifier disabled: execution feedback, no typing, no goal.
\emph{\hermes} is the full verified planner.
\warn{[Pending: \hermes run on CK26 and DB26.]}

\stitle{Prompt overlap}.
\warn{The \hermes{}$^-$ run reported in this draft used an earlier
planner prompt whose action examples named one CK26 entity (an employee
in item 1) and two CK26 properties (\kw{memberOf} and \kw{phone}).
The examples now use an unrelated domain (Figure~\ref{fig-prompt}), and
a rerun with the corrected prompt is in progress; until it finishes,
every \hermes{}$^-$ number in this section is provisional.}
The OneShot and CGS prompts use generic or unrelated examples.

\stitle{Implementation}.
All methods use the same model, Llama~3.1 8B served locally by Ollama
at temperature 0, the same graph stores, and the same scorer.
The planners have a step budget of $B = 15$.
Experiments run on a single Apple-silicon laptop.

\subsection{RQ1: Effectiveness}

Table~\ref{tab-main} reports the finished runs.
On CK26, \hermes{}$^-$ reaches the highest $F_1$ (0.094) and recall
(0.300), against 0.064 and 0.142 for CGS and 0.017 and 0.015 for
OneShot.
It answers 4 items exactly and 37 with non-zero $F_1$
(Figure~\ref{fig-outcomes}).
The ranking holds in both languages: $F_1$ is 0.098 (English) and
0.091 (German) for \hermes{}$^-$, 0.048 and 0.081 for CGS, and 0.007
and 0.027 for OneShot.
Restricting to the 86 items whose gold reproduces changes no
$F_{\mathrm{ndcg}}$ by more than 0.004.
On DB26, where the DBpedia vocabulary is too large to show to the
model, OneShot reaches 0.017, as on CK26.

Table~\ref{tab-features} breaks CK26 down by the feature tags of the
gold queries.

on \kw{COUNT}, \kw{GROUP}, and \kw{LIMIT} is at most 0.03, and all three
score 0 on the six ASK items.
These are exactly the questions whose answer shape (a number, a single
value, a Boolean) a goal states before the query exists, which is what
the verified planner is designed to enforce.

\subsection{RQ2: Components}

\stitle{Reference answers}.
CGS needs a reference answer set.
The gold-oracle ablation replaces the LLM's reference answers with the
gold answers and replays the same cached constraint tables, so it
changes only the reference.
$F_1$ rises from 0.064 to 0.086 and recall from 0.142 to 0.229.
It stays below \hermes{}$^-$, which uses no reference answers, because
41 items fail before the search starts: the model's constraint table
matches nothing in the graph, and no reference can repair that.

\subsection{RQ3: Cost}

Table~\ref{tab-cost} reports the cost of each run, and
Figure~\ref{fig-cost} sets accuracy against model calls.
\hermes{}$^-$ ends with a runnable query on 96 of 100 items, against 59
for CGS and 76 for OneShot, but it spends 1,451 model calls (14.5 per
item, 13.9 steps on average) against 198 for CGS and 116 for OneShot.
The comparison in Table~\ref{tab-main} is therefore not at equal model
budget, and does not by itself show that execution feedback beats
generation with repair.
Graph executions are cheaper: memoization keeps \hermes{}$^-$ at 422
executions, below the 667 of CGS's beam search.

\subsection{Case Study}

\warn{This item is the one the earlier prompt named; the trace will be
replaced by one from the corrected rerun.}
Table~\ref{tab-case} is the trace of \hermes{}$^-$ on the German
version of Example~\ref{exa-motivation}.
Two observations carry the answer.
Joining \kw{?e\ pv{:}memberOf\ ?d} shows 53 employee--department pairs,
so the agent knows the join is correct but unselective; the filter on
the employee found by \kw{lookup} narrows it to one solution, which is
the gold answer.
The trace also shows the limits of an unverified planner: two of its
eight actions are lookups that return nothing.
